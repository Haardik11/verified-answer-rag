Quarterly Business Review - Q3

Executive Summary

Q3 was a strong quarter overall, with total revenue reaching 4.2 million
dollars, a 12 percent increase over the prior year quarter. Growth was
driven primarily by expansion in the enterprise segment, while the
self-serve tier remained roughly flat and the SMB tier saw a slight
seasonal slowdown. Leadership remains confident heading into Q4, though
macro conditions continue to introduce uncertainty into the pipeline.

Revenue by Segment

Enterprise revenue grew 22 percent year over year to 2.1 million dollars,
now representing exactly half of total revenue. This growth was driven by
seven new enterprise logos signed during the quarter, including two
expansion deals with existing customers that upgraded from mid-market
contracts. Average enterprise contract value increased from 145,000
dollars to 168,000 dollars, reflecting both price increases introduced in
Q2 and a shift toward multi-year commitments.

SMB revenue came in at 1.3 million dollars, down 4 percent quarter over
quarter due to typical seasonal purchasing slowdown in the back half of
the year, consistent with the pattern observed in the same period last
year. Churn in the SMB segment ticked up slightly to 3.1 percent monthly,
compared to 2.7 percent in Q2, attributed mostly to two competitors
offering aggressive introductory pricing.

Self-serve revenue was 800,000 dollars, essentially flat compared to Q2.
Signups grew 8 percent, but average revenue per account declined slightly
as a larger proportion of new signups came from the free tier and had not
yet converted to paid plans by quarter end.

Expense Breakdown

Total operating expenses were 2.1 million dollars, flat compared to Q2,
driven mainly by headcount stability across engineering and sales teams -
no net new hires were made in either organization during the quarter,
though several open requisitions remain in the pipeline for Q4.

Cost of goods sold was 620,000 dollars, up slightly from 590,000 dollars
in Q2, due to increased cloud infrastructure spend supporting the new
analytics feature launched in August. Research and development spend was
740,000 dollars. Sales and marketing spend was 520,000 dollars, including
a one-time 80,000 dollar sponsorship of an industry conference in
September. General and administrative expenses were 220,000 dollars.

Headcount and Hiring

Total headcount at quarter end was 142 employees, unchanged from Q2.
Engineering remains the largest function at 58 people, followed by sales
and customer success at 41, and marketing at 18. Two senior engineering
hires that were expected to start in Q3 had their start dates pushed to
early Q4 due to visa processing delays. The company plans to add
approximately 12 net new roles in Q4, concentrated in enterprise sales
and platform engineering.

Customer Metrics

Net revenue retention was 108 percent for the quarter, down from 112
percent in Q2, primarily due to the uptick in SMB churn described above.
Enterprise net revenue retention remained strong at 121 percent. Net
promoter score held steady at 42, consistent with the trailing
four-quarter average. Customer support ticket volume increased 15
percent quarter over quarter, largely attributable to onboarding
questions related to the new analytics feature; median first response
time remained under two hours throughout the quarter.

Product Launches

The most significant product launch of the quarter was the analytics
dashboard, released to general availability in August after a six-week
beta with 40 enterprise customers. Early adoption has been strong, with
31 percent of enterprise accounts enabling the feature within the first
month of general availability. A smaller update to the mobile app,
focused on notification reliability, shipped in September and reduced
reported notification-delivery complaints by roughly 60 percent
month over month.

Risk Factors

The risk factors section notes that revenue recognition timing may shift
up to 3 percent of quarterly revenue between reporting periods due to
contract structure changes introduced this quarter, specifically the
shift toward multi-year enterprise commitments described above, which
are recognized on a different schedule than the annual contracts they
are replacing. Additionally, continued SMB churn at current rates, if
sustained for more than two consecutive quarters, could meaningfully
affect full-year guidance. Macroeconomic conditions, particularly
interest rate uncertainty, continue to be cited by prospects as a factor
lengthening enterprise sales cycles.

Competitive Landscape

Two competitors introduced aggressive discounting in the SMB segment
during Q3, which the team believes is the primary driver of the elevated
SMB churn this quarter. In the enterprise segment, competitive dynamics
were largely unchanged; win rates against the two primary enterprise
competitors held steady at approximately 58 percent in competitive deals
where the outcome was tracked.

Q4 Guidance

Looking ahead to Q4, guidance remains cautious given macro uncertainty,
though the pipeline of enterprise deals in late-stage negotiation
suggests upside potential if even half of them close within the quarter.
Management is targeting Q4 revenue of 4.5 to 4.7 million dollars,
implying 7 to 12 percent quarter-over-quarter growth, with the wide range
reflecting uncertainty in enterprise deal timing.

Capital and Cash Position

The company ended the quarter with 18.4 million dollars in cash and cash
equivalents, providing approximately 22 months of runway at the current
burn rate. No new financing activity occurred during the quarter. The
board approved a refreshed employee equity pool in September, increasing
the available pool by 1.5 percent of fully diluted shares to support
planned Q4 hiring.
